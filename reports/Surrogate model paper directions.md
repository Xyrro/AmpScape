# Make radius transfer the paper, not contrast

The strongest follow-up methods paper is a **scale-consistent learned Omniscape operator**: a T4 surrogate that transfers across window radius and pixel size by construction rather than by retraining. Three pieces of evidence point there. Nobody appears to have published a learned Omniscape surrogate (search-based, not exhaustive). A short derivation from Omniscape's own source code explains AmpScape's transfer failure exactly. And the ecology literature documents coarsening, tiling and block-size compromises that such a model would remove. The globally coupled tasks (T1/T3) are the wrong headline for a main-track methods paper. No verified neural-operator paper trains beyond a coefficient contrast of about 6, while AmpScape runs at 1e6. Learned preconditioners that "beat AMG" do so only when AMG pays setup on every solve, runs on a CPU against a GPU network, or fails outright on non-elliptic matrices. AmpScape already shows two AMG-PCG iterations beating every learned model on T3. Ranked recommendations: (1) the scale-consistent Omniscape operator; (2) a structure-exact, contrast-swept operator for T1/T3 that is honest about where learning breaks; (3) a residual-certified learned/batched solver for the setup-dominated small-window regime. Exact differentiable tools (JAXScape, ConScape sensitivity, radish) are complements and mandatory baselines, not rivals: none of them produces wall-to-wall Omniscape current maps at the scales practitioners report as infeasible. Three checks must come before writing: a direct Google Scholar novelty pass, a numerical check of the radius law (both unverified), and the visibility of the `Xyrro/AmpScape` GitHub repo, which appears in public web search.

Verification tags used below: **[read]** means the researcher read the primary page or PDF text. **[snippet]** means it came from a search-engine extract. **[not fetched]** means a known paper cited from prior knowledge without its numbers. **(derived)** marks the researchers' own arithmetic or derivation, which is not a published claim. AmpScape numbers come from the owner's released benchmark.

## No verified neural operator has trained beyond contrast ~6, and multigrid depth is what wins at low contrast

The 2023-2026 operator literature that best fits AmpScape is the hierarchical/multigrid family. On the Darcy-rough benchmark at 256^2, **MgNO reaches rel-L2 0.339×10^-2 against FNO's 1.613×10^-2 and U-Net's 3.519×10^-2, with 0.57M parameters**. Its own ablation shows that hierarchy depth matters (1.63 → 2.10 → 2.44 going from 5 to 3 levels on NS) ([MgNO arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.19809v3)) [read]. DCNO, which pairs Fourier layers with dilated convolutions, and HANO, with hierarchical attention and an explicit H1 loss, land in the same band (0.531 and 0.556 ×10^-2). DCNO's paper makes the crucial disclosure: **the Darcy-rough coefficient takes values 12 and 2, a contrast of 6** ([DCNO arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00775v1)) [read]. Attention-based geometry operators look worse for AmpScape's problem. LANO reports that "Transolver exhibits large deviations along material interfaces where a(x) changes abruptly", even on the standard FNO Darcy set ([LANO arXiv 2510.16816](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.16816)) [read]. Transolver++, CNO, GNOT and LNO numbers were not retrieved [not fetched / not extracted].

The cross-cutting finding is a gap of roughly five orders of magnitude. **Every verified neural-operator benchmark uses a contrast of order 10^0-10^1. None trains at 1e4-1e6 or with point sources.** High-contrast robustness shows up only where the network sits inside a classical solver. One example is the Cologne group's ML-FETI-DP/ML-AGDSW, where the network only chooses which subdomain edges need eigenproblems ([ML-DDM survey arXiv 2312.14050](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.14050)); the specific contrast values were not extracted. This reframes AmpScape's MgNO divergences (4/6 T4 runs, 2/6 T1 runs). MgNO's paper reports no divergence, but at low contrast ([arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.19809v3)) [read]. The divergences are most plausibly a contrast-scaling pathology rather than an implementation bug (inference, unverified).

Two other families matter for point sources and scale transfer. The 2024-2026 Green's-function work consistently **subtracts the analytically known singular part and learns only a smooth remainder**. Examples are SDC-GON ([arXiv 2609.23206](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23206)), kernel decomposition via a Laplace parametrix ([arXiv 2610.09101](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09101)), and singularity-encoded Green's functions used as preconditioners ([arXiv 2509.11580](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.11580v1)). All three were read at abstract level only, with no error numbers, and all are small continuous domains, not 1e6-contrast graph Laplacians. For size generalisation, the credible route is **local neural solvers inside a domain-decomposition loop**. L-DDM reports a stable **0.645% error on an elliptic domain 50× larger than training** ([arXiv 2507.17328](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.17328)). Mosaic Flow reports Laplace inference on domains **4096× larger** ([arXiv 2308.14258](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.14258)). Scale-consistent learning (Li, Lanthaler, …, Anandkumar) combines rescaling augmentation, a sub-domain consistency loss and a scale-informed operator, with a 34% average error reduction ([arXiv 2507.18813](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.18813)); per-task Darcy numbers were not obtained. Foundation models are not the lever. Poseidon fine-tunes well on Poisson-Gauss, but **CNO trained from scratch beats Poseidon-L on Helmholtz accuracy gain (7.3 vs 6.1)**, and nothing has been evaluated at high contrast ([Poseidon arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.19101v2)) [read].

The answer to question (1): for T1/T3, the best fit is a **deep multigrid operator (about 9 levels at 512^2, as against MgNO's 6 at 256^2) with log-conductance inputs, an H1/gradient loss, analytic singularity subtraction at sources and grounds, and a Kirchhoff-exact output layer**. That layer predicts potentials and computes currents as g_ij(v_i − v_j), following KCLNet's hard-constraint precedent ([arXiv 2506.12902](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.12902)). Wrapping it in DD handles grids beyond 1024^2. None of these ingredients has been verified at 1e6 contrast. For T4 the fit is different: localized-kernel operators with support tied to the radius (Liu-Schiaffini et al., ICML 2024, 34-72% rel-L2 reduction while staying resolution-agnostic ([arXiv 2402.16845](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16845v2)) [read]) plus parameter conditioning (CAPE ([arXiv 2304.14118](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14118v2)), FiLM).

## Learned solvers beat tuned AMG only when AMG pays setup every time

Hybrid methods keep accuracy guarantees in one of two ways. In the first, the network builds something the Krylov loop then checks: a preconditioner with fixed SPD structure, a deflation space, AMG components, or a nonlinear preconditioner inside flexible CG. In the second, a residual-based certificate gates the prediction. What they achieve against a *tuned* AMG-PCG is far less than headline numbers suggest, because most papers compare against Jacobi, IC(0), SSOR or ILU.

| Method | Guarantee mechanism | Result vs AMG | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| NPSDO (Lan et al., ICML 2024) | Fixed-structure preconditioner in CG, inference counted | **0.267 s vs AmgX 0.819 s average; fastest on 95.6% of systems**, but domains change each frame so AMG redoes setup | [arXiv HTML v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.00177v5) [read] |
| Learned SPAI GG^T (Yang et al. 2025) | SPD by construction | **Loses to AmgX+CG on every elliptic case** (Poisson 10 vs 26 ms) in its own appendix | [arXiv 2510.27517](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.27517v1) [read] |
| NeuralIF (TMLR) | Learned IC factor | Ties IC(0) (0.52 vs 0.53 s); no AMG baseline | [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.16368v3) [read] |
| FCG-NO (ICML 2024) | Flexible CG | 20 vs 216 CG iterations at 128^2; no AMG, no wall time | [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2402.05598) [read] |
| Learned AMG smoother (2026 preprint) | AMG hierarchy | 4-37% wall clock vs own AMG; internal inconsistencies | [arXiv 2606.19251](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19251v1) [read] |
| RAPNet (2026 preprint) | Setup-only GNN corrections | 59-71 vs 99-113 iterations (ablation); no BoomerAMG/AmgX | [arXiv 2605.26854](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26854v1) [read] |
| DeepONet deflation (2025) | Deflated PCG | JumpDarcy at contrast 1e5: **1,492 → 1,098 iterations**; ASM, not AMG; no wall time | [arXiv 2508.00101](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.00101) [read] |

The honest reading: **the realistic gain band for "faster solver at fixed accuracy" against AMG is about 4-37% wall clock or 30-40% fewer cycles** (from the learned-AMG-component papers). Large wins come from amortising setup when the operator changes on every solve. NPSDO's advantage over AmgX is that amortisation, not a better asymptotic preconditioner. The strongest non-learned Laplacian competitor is also rarely timed. Approximate Cholesky (Gao, Kyng, Spielman 2023) is benchmarked against BoomerAMG, LAMG and CMG ([arXiv 2303.00709](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.00709v2)) [read]. It ships as `approxchol_lap` in Laplacians.jl, in the same Julia ecosystem as Circuitscape ([GitHub](https://github.com/danspielman/Laplacians.jl/)), and should be timed before any "beats the solver" claim. Default AmgX is not the strongest GPU AMG either: BootCMatchGX beat it in a multi-GPU PCG study ([arXiv 2510.02878](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.02878)) [read].

AmpScape's zero-iteration warm-start result is consistent with this literature, not an anomaly. NOWS reports large savings, but only against Jacobi, SSOR, ICC and ILU, and lists AMG as future work ([arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.02481v4)) [read]. The mechanism (derived, not published as such) is that r0 = A·e0 weights error modes by eigenvalue. A network that removes smooth error but leaves high-frequency error near sources hands AMG a residual it would have cleared in one or two cycles anyway. AmpScape can confirm this cheaply by reporting ||r0||/||b||. Two variants keep guarantees and might do better. One uses the prediction as a deflation/augmentation vector. The other applies HINTS-style smoothing first ([arXiv 2208.13273](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.13273)) [read]. Both are bounded by roughly one AMG iteration (inference), which is too little for a paper unless setup dominates. Certification has the same economics. A residual check plus one V-cycle as an approximate A^-1 norm is cheap, but it costs about as much as the two AMG iterations that already beat the surrogates on T3. Certified surrogates pay off only where setup dominates, which plausibly means many small Omniscape windows (inference; profile first). For those, the non-learned incumbent to beat is **batched GPU Krylov** (Ginkgo batched CG with per-system stopping ([arXiv 2308.08417](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.08417)) [read]) with a cheap preconditioner or reused AMG hierarchies. No paper has benchmarked batched CG on graph-Laplacian windows.

## The Omniscape window operator is open ground, and a one-line law explains the transfer failure

Ten targeted web searches found **no published learned surrogate of Omniscape, moving-window Circuitscape, pairwise Circuitscape, resistant kernels or UNICOR**. The only hit learning Omniscape was AmpScape itself ([GitHub Xyrro/AmpScape](https://github.com/Xyrro/AmpScape)). The nearest ML neighbours solve different problems: CNN surrogates for architectural space-syntax connectivity ([arXiv 1912.12616](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.12616)), random-forest genetic connectivity ([PNAS 2021](https://www.pnas.org/doi/pdf/10.1073/pnas.2003201118)), and inverse landscape genetics ([arXiv 2006.12334](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.12334)). The building blocks exist only separately: Green's-function learners, compact-support kernels, scale-equivariant CNNs ([SESN arXiv 1910.11093](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.11093); [Deep Scale-spaces arXiv 1905.11697](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11697v1)) and parameter conditioning. **None has been combined into a radius-consistent connectivity operator.** The novelty claim therefore holds on the evidence gathered, but it is a search-engine result, not proof. Google Scholar, Web of Science and the ecology journals' own search were not queried. Suggested queries are `"Omniscape" neural`, `"Circuitscape" surrogate` and `"resistant kernel" "neural network"`.

Omniscape.jl v0.6.2 source code reveals structure that a surrogate can exploit or must respect ([utils.jl](https://github.com/Circuitscape/Omniscape.jl/blob/main/src/utils.jl); [main.jl](https://github.com/Circuitscape/Omniscape.jl/blob/main/src/main.jl)) [read]. Targets sit on a block_size grid, each carrying its block's summed source strength. Each window clips resistance to a disk of radius `radius+buffer`, zeroes the target's own block, rescales in-disk sources to sum to the target's amps, grounds the target, and scatter-adds the absolute current. The artefact correction (`correct_artifacts`, on by default) is a **fixed, resistance-independent multiplicative kernel** computed once on a homogeneous disk. Normalized current is `cum_currmap ./ fp_cum_currmap`, where flow potential is the same computation with resistance set to 1. Two consequences follow. First, everything except the windowed solve |J[g|_D, s̃_t]| is exact bookkeeping that a surrogate can copy. Second, because each window is an absolute value with its own grounding, **Omniscape is not a single linear Green's operator on the source field**, which rules out a pure global Green's-function surrogate.

The researchers' derivation (derived, unpublished) explains AmpScape's transfer failure. In a homogeneous interior with uniform source density s, Gauss's law gives radial current A(1−ρ²/r²) across radius ρ. Summing over targets gives **C(x) ≈ (2/3)·s·r: linear in radius and independent of block size**. The rel-L2 error of predicting C at r_train when the truth is at r_eval is then exactly **1 − r_train/r_eval**, the AmpScape observation. Dividing by the radius ratio is the first-order correction, which is why it recovers to 0.19-0.32. Flow potential obeys the same law, so **C/F is O(1) and radius-invariant to leading order**. The radius-dependent residual is the heterogeneity-driven channelisation that the model actually has to learn. With pixel width Δ, the same law gives C ≈ (2/3)σΔR at fixed physical radius. This yields a test-time resampling rule, C_r ≈ λ^-1·U_λ[C_{r/λ}(coarsened inputs)], with which a model trained at r = 64 could serve r = 256. Several invariances follow exactly from the code. Window currents are invariant to a global conductance scale, the map is linear in a global source scale, and translation equivariance breaks only where windows are clipped at raster edges. Together these fix the output's units and can be hard-coded. **No published Omniscape radius-scaling law or sensitivity study was found**, and the heterogeneous case has no closed form. Whether C/F stays radius-stable on real resistance surfaces is the central empirical question.

The answer to question (3) is yes, plausibly novel, with one caveat. A per-window learned operator plus exact aggregation still needs N_T ≈ (H/b)(W/b) network calls, so its speed comes from amortising the window sum into one fully convolutional pass. The window operator is most valuable as a structural prior, as a source of N_T nearly free training pairs per Omniscape run (that requires instrumenting Omniscape to dump window maps), and as the mechanism that makes block size, buffer, threshold and edge clipping exact.

## Practitioners already coarsen, tile and drop species because of compute

Demand for a fast surrogate is documented in first-party sources. The Circuitscape developers' white paper states that "authors using Circuitscape often state that their input data needed to be coarsened to achieve reasonable run times". It also says Lawler et al. coarsened to **50 km cells for a 2,903-species analysis** because it "required running Circuitscape thousands of times" ([Circuitscape white paper](https://circuitscape.org/pubs/circuitscape_whitepaper.pdf)) [read]. National products are tiled or floored. Canada's 300 m map needed **17 tiles of at most 40 Mpx** ([Pither et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9946242/)) [read]. Alberta could not go **below 100 m even with GFlow on the Niagara supercomputer** ([Marrec et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7176682/)) [read]. Brennan et al.'s global Science analysis resampled human footprint from 1 km² to about 70 km² to cut Circuitscape time "from months" ([bioRxiv v2](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.08.16.456503v2.full.pdf)) [snippet; check the published supplement]. Omniscape specifically is the bottleneck. In issue #92 (still open), its author found **doubling from 16 to 32 physical cores gave about 20% speed-up** and concluded "continental-scale analyses may not be possible at this time" ([Omniscape.jl #92](https://github.com/Circuitscape/Omniscape.jl/issues/92)) [read]. This matches AmpScape's own 1.6-1.9× on 24 threads. Dertien & Baldwin report Omniscape at about **140 h on 55 CPUs and 350 GB versus under 2 h for local Circuitscape** ([Frontiers 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/conservation-science/articles/10.3389/fcosc.2023.976914/full)) [snippet]. Belote et al. set Omniscape block size to **10% of the radius** to cut processing time on a 48-vCPU, 384 GB AWS node ([Landscape Ecology 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10980-022-01530-9)) [snippet]. The Omniscape "omnifunctional" preprint clusters 93 species into three guilds because many-species dispersal is "computationally prohibitive" ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.02.03.932095v1.full)) [snippet].

Many-query workloads multiply all of this. ResistanceGA optimisation needs **2,250-13,500 Circuitscape runs per surface**, and its authors call time "the greatest impediment" ([Peterman preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/007575v1.full)) [snippet; confirm against the MEE version]. Its Julia guide warns that CHOLMOD "can be VERY memory intensive" ([Julia_Guide.Rmd](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wpeterman/ResistanceGA/master/vignettes/Julia_Guide.Rmd)) [read]. Sensitivity analysis was "prohibitively slow" before Circuitscape 5 ([Anantharaman et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.03542)) [read]. Marrec's 8-map factorial design shows why scenario sweeps matter: **accepted modelling alternatives correlate cell-wise at only r = 0.34-0.94** ([PMC7176682](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7176682/)) [read].

Accuracy requirements favour AmpScape's finding that rankings survive where magnitudes fail. Practitioners consume z-score cut-offs, quantile classes and pinch-point locations. They accept block_size and tiling approximations described as "negligible" or "highly correlated" ([Pelletier et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3907452/)) [read]. **No published numeric tolerance exists.** A defensible target, the researchers' proposal rather than published guidance, is surrogate-vs-solver agreement well above the 0.34-0.94 spread between accepted alternatives, with top-quantile overlap matching or beating block_size coarsening at equal speed-up. Block_size coarsening is the incumbent cheap approximation and the natural speed-accuracy comparator.

## JAXScape and ConScape are baselines and label sources, not rivals

JAXScape is the most direct "why not an exact differentiable GPU solver" objection. It is MIT-licensed, version 0.0.10 (2026-10-06), and has no accompanying paper. It reports **74× over Circuitscape.jl cg+amg and 17× over CHOLMOD for resistance distance at 1000×1000 on one RTX 3090**, and 32× over ResistanceGA with about 16× lower RMSE on 100×100 inverse landscape genetics ([GitHub](https://github.com/vboussange/jaxscape); [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/15267703)) [read, README claims]. Its outputs are distances (LCP, resistance, RSP), not Circuitscape/Omniscape current-density maps. Its largest stated benchmark is 10^6 cells, and the speed-up compares a GPU against CPU tools. ConScape (Julia, randomised shortest paths) reduces cost with landmarks ([ConScape.jl docs](https://docs.juliahub.com/ConScape/C8nRa/0.2.3/)). Its 2026 preprint replaces node-removal experiments, which are "computationally infeasible at the spatial resolutions needed", with analytical sensitivity ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.01.05.697654.full.pdf)) [snippet; full text not retrieved]. radish gives exact gradients and Hessians, but only for "rasters with less than 1mil cells" ([GitHub nspope/radish](https://github.com/nspope/radish)) [read]. Circuitscape.jl itself has no GPU option ([options docs](https://docs.circuitscape.org/Circuitscape.jl/latest/options/)) [read], and no GPU port of Circuitscape or Omniscape was found.

The positioning that answers question (5): **exact tools win on single-query precision and local derivatives up to about 10^6 cells. The surrogate's niche is amortised many-query use, finite non-local edits where linear sensitivity breaks down, and wall-to-wall Omniscape maps at extents where no exact GPU tool is benchmarked.** Reviewers will expect three things. The first is JAXScape on the *same GPU* at AmpScape tile sizes, with the break-even query count. The second is approxchol and batched GPU PCG as non-learned speed baselines. The third is a comparison of surrogate gradients against JAXScape autodiff gradients on small tiles, if the paper claims fast sensitivities. Exact differentiable solvers can also supply gradient targets for Sobolev training and confirm the surrogate's top-k screened candidates.

## Three paper ideas, ranked

| Rank | Idea | Novelty claim | Key AmpScape experiment | Main risk | Closest prior work |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Scale-consistent Omniscape operator** | First learned omnidirectional-connectivity surrogate that transfers across radius, pixel size and raster size by construction, from an exact radius law and code-level invariances | Train at one or two radii; evaluate zero-shot at 2×/4× radius and the XL tier; close the 0.19-0.32 rescaled gap toward in-tier 0.04-0.08; report ranking/top-quantile metrics on real resistance surfaces vs block_size coarsening and batched-GPU/JAXScape at matched wall time | C/F not radius-stable on heterogeneous surfaces; reviewers call the core a normalisation trick | Scale-consistent learning ([2507.18813](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.18813)); localized kernels ([2402.16845](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16845v2)); CAPE; SESN; AmpScape T4 |
| 2 | **Where neural operators break: structure-exact surrogates across a contrast sweep** | First operator study trained end-to-end from contrast 1e1 to 1e6 with point sources; Kirchhoff-exact outputs plus singularity subtraction as the fix | Sweep contrast on T1/T3; locate the divergence/accuracy cliff for MgNO, DCNO, U-Net; show the structured model moves it and stops divergence; report against 2-iteration AMG-PCG honestly | Even a fixed model stays behind AMG on T3, so main-track reviewers see a negative result | MgNO; DCNO/HANO; SDC-GON; KCLNet; ML-FETI-DP |
| 3 | **Certified amortised solves for many small windows** | Learned/amortised setup (RAPNet-style) plus residual-certified batched GPU PCG for 1e6-contrast graph-Laplacian windows, timed honestly vs tuned AMG, approxchol and Ginkgo batched CG | Profile Omniscape window cost by radius; show setup dominance at small r; compare end-to-end T4 maps at a guaranteed tolerance | Gains capped near the 4-37% band; a non-learned batched solver wins; weak ML-venue appeal | NPSDO; RAPNet; Luz 2020; Ginkgo batched; DeepONet deflation |

**Idea 1 ranks first** because it attacks AmpScape's sharpest, best-explained failure with a mechanism that is falsifiable cheaply. Run the radius law on homogeneous rasters at several r and b before anything else. The architecture would predict normalized current C/F with radius/pixel-size FiLM conditioning and a receptive field tied to 2r. It would multiply by an analytic or separately learned flow potential F (resistance-free, so nearly fixed-kernel) and enforce the conductance-scale and source-linearity invariances exactly. Test-time resampling is the free ablation. The like-for-like speed story is already strong (14,000-23,000× vs the 24-thread solver, break-even after 37-152 maps), and the ecology demand evidence is unusually concrete. The paper must be honest on three points: T4 degrades 1.3-1.5× on real surfaces, magnitudes are weaker than rankings, and resistant kernels (UNICOR) share the window-sum structure, which offers a generality claim if time allows.

**Idea 2** is the higher-ceiling ML contribution and the riskier one. The contrast-sweep finding stands on its own, because no verified paper goes past contrast ~6. But the main track needs the structured model to clearly move the cliff, and AmpScape already shows AMG wins T3 in two iterations. It works best as a substantial section of Idea 1's paper (T1 as a stress test) or as a fallback.

**Idea 3** keeps the strongest guarantees but the literature caps its upside. It belongs in a numerical-methods venue unless profiling shows that setup dominates Omniscape windows by a wide margin. Even then, its batched-solver baseline should appear in Idea 1 regardless.

Two directions to avoid: a learned warm-start paper, where the literature and AmpScape agree the savings against AMG are near zero, and foundation-model fine-tuning, where from-scratch CNO already competes.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the follow-up from "a better operator for hard elliptic solves" to "an operator whose scale behaviour is derived rather than learned". AmpScape's 1 − r_train/r_eval failure is not noise. It is the leading term of a radius law that Omniscape's own source code implies. A model that factors that law out leaves only the bounded channelisation field to learn, which is exactly where U-Nets already succeed in-tier. That turns a benchmark weakness into the paper's central, testable claim. It also lines up with what practitioners need: rank-faithful wall-to-wall maps at native resolution in a regime where exact GPU tools are unbenchmarked and Omniscape's CPU scaling has stalled.

Three uncertainties gate everything. The radius law and C/F stability are derived but unchecked. Novelty rests on web search rather than a Scholar/WoS pass. And the GitHub repo's apparent public visibility threatens double-blind review and should be resolved with the owner before submission. Several demand figures used for motivation (Brennan's 70 km², ResistanceGA solve counts, Dertien & Baldwin, Belote) are [snippet]-level and need primary-text confirmation before they go into the paper.
