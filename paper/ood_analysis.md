# OOD analysis — per-split degradation of the learned baselines (Phase 12, brief §14)

Regenerated by `scripts/paper_figures.py` (F7): `paper/tables/ood_degradation.md`, `paper/figures/ood_degradation.png`.
Numbers below are rel-L2 on the training tier's splits for the official configurations, mean over the three seeds
(`docs/tables/baselines_full.md`); ratios are relative to `test_id`. Scale transfer (XL/XXL) is treated separately in
`paper/tables/scale_transfer.md` and `docs/status/latest.md`.

## Findings

1. **Held-out resistance table and held-out contrast (`test_ood`) cost little at the training tier.** Ratios are
   0.92–1.18 for every model and task at S–L except the ViT on T1 at L (1.49, its structurally failing config). T4 is
   the most sensitive (1.04–1.18): the learned Omniscape surrogates lose more on an unseen table than the pairwise
   surrogates do. [paper/tables/ood_degradation.md]
2. **The held-out-region split (`ood_region`) is *easier* than `test_id` for every model, task and tier** (ratios
   0.65–1.00; typically 0.70–0.85). This is not domain robustness: the held-out biomes (montane grasslands and
   shrublands, mangroves) and realm (Australasia) produce landscapes with different composition — see the table below —
   and the error of all models tracks landscape difficulty more than spatial novelty. The paper must present
   `ood_region` as a *spatial-leakage control* (test regions never overlap training regions), not as a stress test, and
   compare splits on matched difficulty where a robustness claim is made.
3. **Published real resistance surfaces are the hardest in-tier set for T4** (S only: ratios 1.30–1.52; U-Net 1.52),
   while for T1/T3 they are within ±15 % of `test_id`. The T4 surrogates are the ones that would be used on
   practitioners' own surfaces, so this is the number to quote beside the headline.
4. **Rankings degrade less than magnitudes** (Spearman and top-5 % IoU columns in `docs/tables/baselines_full.md`),
   consistent with the scale-transfer finding.
