# Practitioner demand for a fast surrogate of circuit-theoretic connectivity (Circuitscape / Omniscape), and documented compute bottlenecks

Notes compiled 2026-10-19. Verification legend: **[read]** = I read the primary text (full-text XML/PDF or page fetch); **[snippet]** = taken from a search-engine extract of the cited page, not read in full (verify before citing in the paper). The pre-known items (Dertien & Baldwin 2023: about 140 h, 55 CPUs, 350 GB per species; Heckford bc-connectivity: 196 h; Dickson et al. 2019: 459 applications) are not repeated except where I found something new.

## 1. Many-query workflows: how many solves, and what compute is reported

### Takeaway
Resistance-surface optimisation is the clearest many-query workload: a typical ResistanceGA run needs thousands of full Circuitscape solves (2,250–13,500), and its own documentation calls compute time the greatest limitation. Sensitivity and uncertainty analyses, scenario sweeps and multi-species work multiply the count of wall-to-wall solves. The literature repeatedly says these were "prohibitively slow" or "computationally prohibitive", or that they forced coarsening.

### Cited Findings
- **ResistanceGA (Peterman 2018, MEE).** "A typical optimization takes 50–300 generations, resulting in the creation of 2,250–13,500 resistance surfaces and CIRCUITSCAPE/gdistance runs. As such, the greatest impediment to optimizing resistance surfaces is time." On an Intel i7 3.4 GHz machine, one iteration with 25 samples on a 250×250 surface takes about 5 s, and on a 1,000×1,000 surface up to about 90 s. **[snippet; bioRxiv preprint v1]** — [Peterman bioRxiv preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/007575v1.full); published version: [Peterman 2018 MEE](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/2041-210X.12984)
  - Implied cost: 13,500 runs × 90 s ≈ 337 h of serial compute for one optimisation of one surface at only 1 Mpx. This is my arithmetic, not a reported figure.
  - The published paper reports that commute-time (gdistance) is on average 2.1× faster than Circuitscape. It also suggests parallel GA and 4-neighbour connections to cut runtime. **[snippet]** — [Peterman 2018 MEE](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/2041-210X.12984)
- **ResistanceGA's Julia guide** (package vignette): "One of the greatest limitations of using ResistanceGA for landscape resistance optimization is computation time. This time increases as the number of samples in space increases and/or the dimensions of the raster surface increase." It states that Julia Circuitscape "can be 4–10x faster" than the Python version. It warns that the CHOLMOD default (where the speed comes from) "can be VERY memory intensive, so may not be practical if dealing with large rasters", and suggests an *experimental* single-precision iterative mode as the alternative. **[read: raw vignette from GitHub]** — [ResistanceGA Julia_Guide.Rmd](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wpeterman/ResistanceGA/master/vignettes/Julia_Guide.Rmd)
- **radish (Pope & Peterman, R package v0.0.2, 2020-05-05).** A gradient-based maximum-likelihood alternative to the GA, with fast gradients and Hessians. It is explicitly limited to "moderate-sized problems (e.g. rasters with less than 1mil cells, where a sparse Cholesky decomposition of the graph Laplacian is feasible)". **[snippet of the package README/DESCRIPTION]** — [radish GitHub](https://github.com/nspope/radish). I found no published head-to-head speed benchmark against ResistanceGA (see Gaps).
- **Sensitivity analysis as standard practice, and too slow before CS 5.** Anantharaman et al. (Circuitscape.jl paper): Julia CS "enables users to experiment with different scenarios and perform sensitivity analysis, which is used by most users to understand and validate the effect of all the individual layers that go into a resistance map. With previous versions, practical sensitivity testing using Circuitscape was prohibitively slow." **[read]** — [Anantharaman et al. 2019, arXiv:1906.03542](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.03542)
- **Uncertainty analysis, Alberta (Marrec et al. 2020, Sci Rep 10:6798).** This is a factorial design of 2×2×2 = 8 wall-to-wall current-density maps (H-index × water resistance 0/1000 × scaling function), run with GFlow at 100 m. Cell-by-cell correlations between the maps ranged from **r = 0.34 to 0.94**: resistance choices change the outputs a great deal, so many-scenario runs are needed. Water resistance explained 42.4% and scaling 28.4% of the among-map variance. **[read]** — [Marrec et al. 2020 (PMC7176682)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7176682/)
  - Each map was itself a many-solve run: GFlow solved pairs drawn at random from 50 nodes until the convergence criterion reached 0.999. **[read]** — same source.
- **Original call for uncertainty analyses.** McRae et al. 2008 say resistance values are always uncertain and urge uncertainty analyses of each modelling decision. **[snippet]** — [McRae et al. 2008 Ecology](https://circuitscape.org/pubs/McRae_et_al_2008_Ecology.pdf)
- **Multi-species is "computationally prohibitive".** The Omniscape "omnifunctional" preprint (Landau/McClure/Dickson et al.) states that "simulating dispersal for many species is computationally prohibitive" and works around it by clustering 93 species into three dispersal guilds. **[snippet]** — [bioRxiv 2020.02.03.932095](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.02.03.932095v1.full)
- **2,903-species climate-movement study forced to 50 km cells.** The Circuitscape white paper (McRae, Shah, Edelman; TNC/Julia Computing/MIT) says Lawler et al. "coarsened their human impact data to 50 km x 50 km grid cells, because their analysis required running Circuitscape thousands of times." **[read]** — [Circuitscape white paper](https://circuitscape.org/pubs/circuitscape_whitepaper.pdf)
- **Climate-scenario sweeps.** Drake et al. (Sonoran Desert water sources for mule deer vs invasive bullfrogs, under current and future climate scenarios): "Runs required 4-8 weeks on a Janus supercomputer at Texas Tech; coarsened data to improve speed." **[read: TNC/NASA presentation slide]** — [Hall (TNC) NASA Biodiversity 2019 slides](https://cce.nasa.gov/biodiversity/pdf/meetings/bio2019/wed/bo1_Ca_Circuitscape%20NASA%20presentation%20May%2022%202019%20part%201%20Hall-compressed.pdf). The same dataset's all-to-one run took more than 2 days in Python CS 4 and under 3 h in Julia CS 5 (about 18×). **[read]** — [Anantharaman et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.03542)
- **Multi-time-step climate connectivity with Omniscape** (time series of SDM-derived inputs). One study used five periods (1970–2000, 2021–2040, 2041–2060, 2061–2080, 2081–2100) × three SSPs (126/370/585) at about 300 m with Omniscape v0.6.2, i.e. 13 runs per species. **[snippet; exact paper not identified in the extract — verify]** — surfaced via search for Omniscape climate time steps. Treat as unverified until the paper is pinned down.
- **California "no-regrets" climate connectivity (Cameron et al. 2022, Conserv Sci Pract).** Omniscape with a 50 km moving window; two GCMs (HADGEM-ES, CNRM-CM5) plus a uniform-resistance "null" model, i.e. at least 4 statewide Omniscape runs. No compute was reported in the text I read. **[read]** — [PMC9285781](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9285781)
- **Linkage Mapper / Pinchpoint Mapper (restoration and corridor workflows).** A Great Lakes coastline run (31,420,841 cells at 30 m; 1,110 core nodes; earlier attempt with about 374 cores) failed with MemoryError after **29 h 59 min**, and a later attempt failed after **166 h 39 min**. Brad McRae: "your computer doesn't have enough RAM to run Circuitscape with this large of a study area"; the raster-centrality option "is especially memory intensive". **[read: Google Groups thread, Jan–Feb 2017]** — [linkage-mapper forum](https://groups.google.com/g/linkage-mapper/c/NMRddl8TVBU/m/eMeYR7_HAgAJ)
- **Linkage Mapper still tied to Python CS 4.** There is an open issue to upgrade the Circuitscape-dependent tools (Pinchpoint, Centrality) to CS 5 (Julia). **[snippet]** — [linkage-mapper issue #103](https://github.com/linkagescape/linkage-mapper/issues/103/)
- **Node count makes models infeasible.** South Atlantic LCC connectivity models (90 m): a box turtle model "proved to be computationally infeasible given the sheer number of nodes". **[snippet]** — [ScienceBase item](https://sciencebase.gov/catalog/item/538786a2e4b0aa26cd7b5476)

### Inferences
- Solve counts per workflow, using the figures above: GA optimisation needs 10^3–10^4 solves per surface. A factorial sensitivity design needs 2^k wall-to-wall maps (8 in Marrec). Climate × time × species sweeps need 10^1–10^3 maps, and each Omniscape map is itself 10^5–10^7 local solves. A surrogate pays off most here, because the per-solve cost multiplies.
- ResistanceGA and radish users currently pick small rasters (≤ 1 Mpx for radish) or coarse grain because of solve cost. That is direct evidence that resolution is traded against the number of queries.

### Gaps
- I did not find a paper that reports the **total** CPU hours of a full ResistanceGA landscape-genetics study. Only the per-iteration figures from the preprint are available.
- No speed benchmark for radish vs ResistanceGA was found.
- **Bowman et al. 2020** and the **Beier et al.** sensitivity papers named in the brief were not located or verified in this pass. Do not cite them without checking.
- Barrier Mapper runtimes were not found. Linkage Mapper evidence is limited to one 2017 forum thread.
- The ResistanceGA preprint figures (2,250–13,500 runs; 5 s and 90 s per iteration) come from a search extract of the bioRxiv page (fetch returned 429). Confirm them against the published MEE paper before quoting.

## 2. Large-scale applications: runtimes, HPC use and resolution compromises

### Takeaway
Every national or continental circuit-theory map I found either ran on HPC or cloud nodes (48 vCPU / 384 GB AWS; Niagara supercomputer; university clusters) or was compromised to fit compute: coarsening of up to 70× (Brennan 2022, global), tiling (Canada in 17 tiles), or a 100 m floor (Alberta).

### Cited Findings
- **Global protected-area connectivity (Brennan et al. 2022, Science 376:1101).** The preprint states the authors resampled the human footprint index "from 1 km^2 to ~70 km^2 … to reduce computation time in our Circuitscape analysis from months to" [the extract is truncated; it continues with a shorter timescale]. **[snippet of the bioRxiv v2 preprint; full PDF blocked]** — [Brennan et al. bioRxiv v2](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.08.16.456503v2.full.pdf); [Science DOI](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abl8974). Check whether the published Science version kept the ~70 km² grain.
  - A follow-up (sentinel nodes, PMC10612492) notes that Brennan's index needs Circuitscape run twice (current map plus one-to-all for the index), which adds to cost. **[snippet]** — [PMC10612492](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10612492/)
- **North America (Belote et al. 2022, Landscape Ecology).** Omniscape v0.5.8 on an AWS r5a.12xlarge (**48 vCPUs, 384 GB RAM**). Radii of 30, 150, 300 and 700 km, and block size set at **10% of the window radius** to cut processing time "with minimal conspicuous spatial artifacts". Outputs from different block sizes were "all highly correlated". **[snippet; Springer page redirected to login]** — [Belote et al. 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10980-022-01530-9). Runtime not captured; check the full text or Supplement 2.
- **Canada (Pither et al. 2023, PLoS ONE 18:e0281980).** 300 m national map. "Computational limitations meant that the largest tile we could efficiently analyse was 3,600,000 km² (40 million pixels). This required us to divide Canada into 17 rectangular tiles". Four directional runs per tile (N–S, S–N, E–W, W–E). Their tile-size sensitivity analysis found that current-density variation became negligible above 150,000 km² (1.7 Mpx). An earlier Canada-wide study at 25 m "required dividing Canada into thousands of small tiles (25 x 25 km) due to computational limitations". Koen et al. showed that accuracy of current density declines with smaller tiles. **[read]** — [Pither et al. 2023 (PMC9946242)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9946242/)
  - Policy use: the map is positioned for Canada's national Ecological Corridor Program and the 2 Billion Trees Program, and for the Kunming-Montreal GBF connectivity targets. **[read]** — same source.
- **Alberta, Marrec et al. 2020.** "Using GFlow on the Niagara supercomputing facility (SciNet HPC Consortium), we still encountered technical limitations that prevented us from modelling current density at a spatial resolution below 100 m across the province". Resistance was computed at 10 m and then aggregated to 100 m. Alberta is 661,848 km², and the Alberta Land-use Framework / ABMI want a connectivity index. **[read]** — [PMC7176682](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7176682/)
- **Québec / Montérégie tiling (Pelletier et al. 2014, PLoS ONE).** "Because its technical demands increase with the number of land-surface pixels being considered, circuit modeling runs are constrained to limited raster sizes: relatively small areas or large areas at coarsened spatial resolutions." Tiles with buffers and directional sweeps were used to build arbitrarily large mosaics. **[read]** — [Pelletier et al. 2014 (PMC3907452)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3907452/). A Québec regional project needing "an estimated total of 3 core-years" was surfaced in a search extract (attributed to Hall et al. 2021 / Land) **[snippet; unverified, source page returned 403]**.
- **Mojave desert tortoise (Gray & Chang 2019).** A 437-million-pixel resistance surface crashed Python CS 4; Julia CS 5 completed it. **[read]** — [Anantharaman et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.03542)
- **Omniscape scaling ceiling (Omniscape.jl issue #92, opened 2021-03-09 by Omniscape author vlandau).** Window of 668 px (about 1.4 Mpx per solve). On Azure VMs (64 logical / 32 physical cores, 256 GB): 63 threads gave about 5 h 15 min and 32 threads about 3 h 45 min. On 32 logical / 16 physical: 31 threads about 4 h 45 min and 16 threads about 4 h 35 min. Doubling from 16 to 32 physical cores gave about 20% speed-up. Author: "**continental-scale analyses may not be possible at this time given these numbers.**" Later users found 8–12 threads (and 6 threads on multi-day runs) to be the sweet spot. The issue is still open. **[read]** — [Omniscape.jl #92](https://github.com/Circuitscape/Omniscape.jl/issues/92)
- **Central India tigers (Dutta et al. 2015).** "Constraint – had to coarsen habitat data from 24 to 72 m". **[read: slide]** — [Hall (TNC) NASA 2019 slides](https://cce.nasa.gov/biodiversity/pdf/meetings/bio2019/wed/bo1_Ca_Circuitscape%20NASA%20presentation%20May%2022%202019%20part%201%20Hall-compressed.pdf)
- **General scale threshold.** "Datasets with more than ~20 million grid cells typically require workstations or cloud computing resources." **[read]** — [Circuitscape white paper](https://circuitscape.org/pubs/circuitscape_whitepaper.pdf). Older guidance put the limit of CS 4 at roughly 2–6 Mpx on 32-bit systems and said "coarser cell sizes may be needed". **[snippet]** — [Circuitscape 4.0 User Guide](https://circuitscape.org/downloads/circuitscape_4_0.pdf)
- **Dertien & Baldwin 2023 (Frontiers Conserv Sci) comparator.** On the Clemson Palmetto cluster, local Circuitscape took < 2 h (4 CPU, 16 GB) and regional Circuitscape < 1 h (8 CPU, 120 GB), against Omniscape at about 140 h (55 CPU, 350 GB). The Omniscape plan "came at the cost of time and computational resources". **[snippet]** — [Dertien & Baldwin 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/conservation-science/articles/10.3389/fcosc.2023.976914/full). Omniscape costs about 100× more than pairwise Circuitscape per species here.

### Inferences
- National and continental products (Canada, Alberta, North America, global) are being made for policy (GBF Target 3/connectivity, Canada's Ecological Corridor Program, Alberta Land-use Framework). All of them report coarsening, tiling, or cloud/HPC nodes with 384 GB of RAM. A surrogate that runs at native resolution on one GPU would remove a compromise that is already documented.
- The 70× coarsening in Brennan 2022 (Science) is the strongest single "resolution traded for compute" example, but its exact wording must be verified against the published supplement.

### Gaps
- UK (Natural England / Biodiversity Net Gain / Local Nature Recovery Strategies), US State Wildlife Action Plan and USFWS compute reports: **not found** in this pass. I cannot confirm that these programmes run Circuitscape at scale.
- Pacific Northwest (McRae et al. 2016, "Conserving Nature's Stage"; TNC Resilient and Connected Landscapes) runtimes were not found. The ResearchGate record surfaced but was not read.
- Belote et al. 2022 total runtime and Brennan et al. 2022 total runtime were not captured.

## 3. Evidence that practitioners lower resolution, raise block_size, or limit species/scenarios because of compute

### Takeaway
There is explicit, repeated, first-party evidence: the Circuitscape developers' white paper says users "often state that their input data needed to be coarsened". Omniscape's own docs recommend block_size coarsening, and published continental work set block size by compute. Species are clustered into guilds, and models were dropped as infeasible.

### Cited Findings
- "Authors using Circuitscape often state that their input data needed to be coarsened to achieve reasonable run times, compromising the ability to discern the effects of fine-scaled landscape features." **[read]** — [Circuitscape white paper](https://circuitscape.org/pubs/circuitscape_whitepaper.pdf)
- Resolution examples: 1 km → ~70 km² (Brennan 2022, global) [snippet]; 50 km cells for 2,903 species (Lawler et al., via the white paper) [read]; 24 → 72 m (Dutta 2015 tigers) [read]; 10 → 100 m floor on a supercomputer (Marrec 2020 Alberta) [read]; 4–8 weeks on a supercomputer even after coarsening (Drake, Sonoran) [read]. Sources as in §§1–2.
- Block size: circuitscaper (the R wrapper on CRAN) describes `block_size = 3` as coarsening the source grid into 3×3 blocks, "reducing the number of solves (and thus computation time) substantially with typically negligible effects on results". **[snippet]** — [circuitscaper os_run docs](https://rdrr.io/cran/circuitscaper/man/os_run.html). The Omniscape.jl example says visible blocking "is an artifact of setting a large block_size to make the example run faster". **[snippet]** — [Omniscape.jl examples](https://docs.circuitscape.org/Omniscape.jl/latest/examples/). Belote 2022 used block = 10% of radius, and another study used a 45-pixel block with a 50 km window "to reduce computational complexity". **[snippet]** — [Belote 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10980-022-01530-9)
- Species limits: 93 species clustered into 3 guilds because many-species dispersal is "computationally prohibitive" (Omniscape omnifunctional preprint) [snippet]. Box turtle model dropped as "computationally infeasible" (South Atlantic LCC) [snippet]. Sources as in §1.
- Directional-run limits: Pelletier et al. 2014 found two directional runs (rather than four) captured the meaningful variability. This is a cost-saving simplification. **[read]** — [PMC3907452](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3907452/)
- Tiling as a compute workaround, with accuracy cost (Pither 2023: 17 tiles; thousands of 25 km tiles in an earlier 25 m Canadian study; accuracy declines with smaller tiles per Koen et al.). **[read]** — [PMC9946242](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9946242/)

### Inferences
- The block_size "negligible effect" claim is a soft statement from documentation, not a quantified benchmark. Belote's "highly correlated" outputs are the closest published check. A surrogate paper could position itself against block_size coarsening as the incumbent cheap approximation and compare errors.

### Gaps
- No systematic survey quantifying how often published studies coarsened for compute (the white paper statement is qualitative).

## 4. Existing acceleration attempts and their limits

### Takeaway
The acceleration so far is CPU-side: the Julia rewrite (4×–18× on benchmarks), gflow (PETSc/HYPRE-based, for HPC), multithreaded Omniscape (scaling stalls at about 8–16 threads) and ConScape (landmarks). I found no GPU solver or learned surrogate for Circuitscape/Omniscape. Memory (CHOLMOD) and thread scaling are the remaining limits.

### Cited Findings
- **Circuitscape.jl (CS 5).** Up to 1,800% speed-up over CS 4.0.5, scaling to 437 Mpx. Benchmarks used a Xeon Silver 4114 with 384 GB RAM. All-to-one real dataset: > 2 days → < 3 h (about 18×). It adds tile-level parallel batches for country/continent runs. **[read]** — [Anantharaman et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.03542). The README gives a more conservative "up to 4x faster … on 16 parallel processes" on standard benchmarks. **[snippet]** — [Circuitscape.jl GitHub](https://github.com/Circuitscape/Circuitscape.jl)
- **CHOLMOD memory limit.** "can be VERY memory intensive, so may not be practical if dealing with large rasters" (ResistanceGA Julia guide). **[read]** — [Julia_Guide.Rmd](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wpeterman/ResistanceGA/master/vignettes/Julia_Guide.Rmd)
- **gflow (Leonard et al. 2017, MEE 8:519–526).** A C/PETSc tool for HPC: 173× speed-up using HPC and 8.4× on a desktop versus existing software, with reduced memory. **[snippet: abstract]** — [Leonard et al. 2017](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/2041-210X.12689). Even so, the Alberta study could not go below 100 m with gflow on the Niagara supercomputer. **[read]** — [PMC7176682](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7176682/)
- **Omniscape.jl multithreading.** Speed-up saturates (16→32 physical cores gives about 20%), and the author doubts continental feasibility (issue #92, open). **[read]** — [Omniscape.jl #92](https://github.com/Circuitscape/Omniscape.jl/issues/92)
- **Cloud runs.** Belote 2022 used AWS r5a.12xlarge [snippet], and the issue #92 benchmarks used Azure VMs [read].
- **ConScape (Van Moorter et al. 2023, MEE).** A randomized-shortest-path library with a landmark approximation for large landscapes. Underestimation of connected habitat grows with coarseness but "remains fairly low even with only few landmarks". **[snippet]** — [Van Moorter et al. 2023](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/2041-210X.13850)
- **Linkage Mapper** tools are still tied to CS 4 (Python) per an open GitHub issue, so many restoration/corridor practitioners do not benefit from the CS 5 speed-ups. **[snippet]** — [linkage-mapper #103](https://github.com/linkagescape/linkage-mapper/issues/103/)
- **No GPU or ML surrogate found.** A search for GPU Circuitscape / GNN connectivity emulators returned only circuit-design GNNs (EDA, superconducting circuits) and hydraulic surrogates, none for landscape connectivity. **[search result, absence]** — e.g. [SuperCond-GNN arXiv 2606.23548](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.23548) (unrelated domain, shown only to document the null result)

### Inferences
- The incumbent speed-ups are about one order of magnitude, and they hit memory (direct solvers) or parallel-scaling ceilings. A learned surrogate targets a different regime: amortised many-query cost on a GPU.

### Gaps
- I did not verify whether Circuitscape.jl has an experimental GPU/CUDA solver branch. A quick check of the GitHub issues is recommended before claiming that none exists.
- The ConScape compute numbers were not extracted.

## 5. What accuracy practitioners need

### Takeaway
Practitioners mostly consume **relative** outputs: ranked or quantile-classified current density, z-score cut-offs for "important" cells, and pinch-point and corridor locations. Agreement between alternative maps is judged with cell-wise correlations, and practitioners already accept block_size and tiling approximations described as having "negligible" effects. This argues for rank-, correlation- and top-k-overlap metrics alongside relative L2.

### Cited Findings
- Marrec et al. 2020 classify cells as important with z-score cut-offs (z ≥ 0, 1, 2) and compare maps by Pearson r. They found "higher concordance among cells with high (standardized) current density values than among cells with low values, which supports the identification of cells … based on a cut-off value." Accepted alternatives differed by r = 0.34–0.94. **[read]** — [PMC7176682](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7176682/)
- Pelletier et al. 2014: a well-parameterized connectivity mosaic "provides information for ranking possible movement paths". Mosaics across tile and buffer choices were "nearly identical", differing mainly in seams. "Circuit theory connectivity models are generally seen to be robust to coarsening of input data". **[read]** — [PMC3907452](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3907452/)
- South Atlantic LCC outputs classified into 20 quantile classes. **[snippet]** — [ScienceBase](https://sciencebase.gov/catalog/item/538786a2e4b0aa26cd7b5476)
- Pither 2023 used tile-size sensitivity, where current density variation became "negligible" above 1.7 Mpx tiles, as the accuracy criterion. **[read]** — [PMC9946242](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9946242/)
- Belote 2022: outputs from different block sizes were "all highly correlated", and that was accepted as sufficient. **[snippet]** — [Belote 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10980-022-01530-9)
- In the TNC/Dutta example, high-current areas are treated as "priorities for protection", i.e. location and rank matter, not amperes. **[read: slide]** — [Hall NASA 2019 slides](https://cce.nasa.gov/biodiversity/pdf/meetings/bio2019/wed/bo1_Ca_Circuitscape%20NASA%20presentation%20May%2022%202019%20part%201%20Hall-compressed.pdf)
- Validation is rare: one review found only 7% of studies validated resistance layers and 18% validated connectivity outputs. **[snippet; attributed to "Four steps to strengthen connectivity modeling", Ecography]** — [Ecography 2023](https://nsojournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ecog.06766)
- Resolution itself changes conservation prioritisation (Arponen et al., cited in Marrec 2020), so accuracy at fine grain matters for decisions. **[read, as a secondary citation]** — [PMC7176682](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7176682/)

### Inferences
- An honest accuracy target could be: surrogate-vs-solver agreement should exceed the agreement between accepted modelling alternatives, i.e. cell-wise r well above the 0.34–0.94 range across parameter choices in Marrec 2020. Top-quantile (z ≥ 1, z ≥ 2) overlap and pinch-point location recall should match or beat block_size coarsening at the same speed-up. This is my proposal, not published guidance.

### Gaps
- I found **no published numeric tolerance** (e.g. "rank correlation ≥ 0.9 is acceptable") from agencies or methods papers. The accuracy requirement has to be argued from the indirect evidence above.
